## The science (checked)
- Cats have a mirror-like layer, the **tapetum lucidum**, behind the retina. Light that slips past the retina bounces back, so the retina gets a **second chance** to catch it. That bounced light leaving the eye is the eyeshine you see at night. Humans don't have this layer. (Popular Science; Cats Protection)
- A cat's eye glows only with outside light: it **reflects**, it doesn't make light.
- **Percy Shaw** (Halifax, England) patented reflecting road studs, **"Catseyes", in 1934** and started Reflecting Roadstuds Ltd in 1935. Glass "eyes" send headlight beams back to the driver. A rubber pad with a rainwater dip wipes the glass when a tyre presses the stud down ("closed like an eyelid"). Blackouts in WW2 boosted demand; the factory later made over 1 million a year. (Wikipedia/Percy Shaw; MyLearning – Calderdale Museums)
- **Honesty note:** the cat-on-the-fence story is the famous version, but Shaw told several versions (reflective road signs; tram rails in fog). The script says "One famous story says…" and "He told a few stories, but cats got the fame!"
- Both use the same optics: a **lens in front plus a mirror behind** sends light straight back toward where it came from (retroreflection). Real, deployed technology, still used on roads worldwide.
